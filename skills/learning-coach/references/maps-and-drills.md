# Maps, bootstrap and drills

## Assess a map with GRINDE

- Grouped: organized by forces, tensions or functions, not source order.
- Reflective: shows the learner's model, uncertainty and open questions.
- Interconnected: cross-links between ideas, not isolated clusters.
- Non-linear: position, arrows, labels, sketches or hierarchy instead of prose notes.
- Directional: links name the relationship (causes, depends on, trades off with, degrades into, enables).
- Emphasized: the most important ideas are visibly larger, central or prioritized.

## Critique a map in this order

1. Missing concepts or constraints.
2. Unlabeled or unclear links.
3. Groupings inherited from the source rather than made by the learner.
4. One suspected weak connection, phrased as a question.
5. What the emphasis reveals about the learner's model.

## Bootstrap a new domain

A short model-building pass:
1. Scope the boundary from a syllabus, table of contents, project tree, outline or resource list.
2. Skim headings, diagrams, examples, interfaces or problem types within a hard time cap.
3. Dump keywords in scrambled order, so the learner does not inherit the source's structure.
4. Clarify unknown terms briefly.
5. The learner builds a hypothesis map, and errors in it are expected.

## Drills

Rotate them, and strip obvious cues:
- Force-first recognition: describe the forces and ask what resolves them.
- Inversion: ask when the idea is wrong, harmful or insufficient.
- Compression: explain the idea briefly without jargon.
- Transfer: ask where the same structure appears elsewhere.
- Constraint mutation: change one condition and ask what breaks.
- Design-then-compare: the learner commits to a design before reading the exemplar.
- Pre-mortem: ask what would fail under real pressure.
- Cross-examination: pressure-test assumptions, warmly and directly.
